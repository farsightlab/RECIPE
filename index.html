RECIPE: Procedural Planning via Grounding in Instructional Video

Extracting clean step labels from noisy video is hard; verifying that a generated plan is grounded in a large narrated corpus is cheap. RECIPE turns a noisy instructional-video corpus into a scalable verifier for procedural plans, with no pseudo-labels and no separately trained critic.

Overview

Open-vocabulary visual planning

Given a partial video and a goal, the model must generate the remaining steps of the procedure in natural language, with no fixed action taxonomy: free-form instructions that can be tailored to the tools, ingredients, and cues visible in the video.

The annotation bottleneck

  • Annotated datasets such as CrossTask and COIN are small, domain-narrow, and encode one trajectory per example, although many valid orderings often exist.
  • HowTo100M offers orders of magnitude more procedural content (1.2M videos), but its automatically transcribed narrations are noisy and lack reliable step segmentation.
  • Supervised fine-tuning on pseudo-labels extracted from those narrations propagates segmentation and alignment errors into training, and the supervision is still single-trajectory.

Key insight: verify, don't extract

RECIPE (Reward from Empirical Corpus of Instructional Procedure Examples) reframes the role of the noisy corpus. Rather than mining it for labels or distilling it into a critic model, we use the data itself as the verifier: the history localizes the relevant region of the corpus, and the reward measures how well the generated completion aligns with it. Because many different orderings can each score well, the policy learns from several valid plans at once instead of being pushed toward a single annotator's choice.

Contributions

  • Grounding-as-verification for procedural planning. We turn a noisy, large-scale instructional video corpus into a verifier for generated plans rather than a source of labels. A history baseline credits the policy only for what the continuation adds beyond the partial plan. The reward is optimized with GRPO and applies uniformly whether the planner reads textual histories (Socratic) or raw video tokens (Video).
  • Empirical study. RECIPE-RL improves over the base checkpoint at every scale (0.5B, 3B, 7B) and on all seven in-domain and zero-shot benchmarks. It outperforms supervised fine-tuning on both annotated continuations and HowTo100M pseudo-labels, transfers to a Video planner, and still improves when trained fully unsupervised, without curated procedural datasets.
  • An open-ended evaluation protocol. We aggregate seven procedural-video datasets and score generated plans with a six-criterion LLM-as-judge rubric, validated against human preferences. We will host an online evaluation server alongside the released code.

How the Reward Is Computed

The reward is computed against HowToCaption, a reprocessing of HowTo100M narrations into 25M captions from 1.2M videos. We use only the text, never as labels for the policy, and all alignment happens in a precomputed sentence-embedding space. The worked example below follows one prompt through the three steps.

Toy example with illustrative scores. The reward curve is drawn for A(shist, N) = 0.40; τ = 0.10 and α = 2 as in the paper.

1Sample rollouts

For each prompt the policy samples a group of candidate completions ĉ. Each one forms two text sequences: the history alone, and the history followed by the completion.

2Ground them in the corpus

A fast order-preserving retrieval keeps the top-K narrations; a global Needleman–Wunsch alignment then re-scores them, penalizing inserted or skipped steps.

3Reward the progress

A history baseline credits only the progress beyond what the history already explains. Below a progress gate the plan gets a penalty, so paraphrasing the history does not pay.

Progress that the narrations support is rewarded; copying the history is penalized. The standardized rewards are used as GRPO advantages within each group of rollouts.

Experiments

We evaluate on seven procedural benchmarks: CrossTask, COIN, CaptainCook4D, and EgoProceL in-domain; NIV, Ego-Exo4D, and EgoPER zero-shot. Planners are Qwen2.5 language models at 0.5B, 3B, and 7B in the Socratic configuration, with a frozen Qwen2.5-VL-3B as the vision-language model.

Open-ended evaluation protocol

Open-ended plans preclude exact-match metrics, so we adopt a reference-based LLM-as-judge protocol with Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct. The judge scores each continuation on six 0–5 criteria: logical progression, temporal alignment, spatial grounding, continuation, clarity, and semantic alignment. We report the rubric score (%): per-example scores normalized by the 30-point maximum, averaged within each dataset, then averaged across the datasets of a split.

We validate the judge against human preferences on 178 examples (103 in-domain, 75 zero-shot) with 13 raters comparing continuations from five training schemes.

RECIPE-RL improves at every scale

RECIPE-RL improves over the base checkpoint at every scale (0.5B, 3B, 7B) and on every benchmark, with rubric-score gains of +7 to +8 points in-domain and up to +16 points zero-shot. At 7B, in-domain and zero-shot scores are within 0.5 points (46.6 vs. 46.1), indicating the gains do not come at the cost of generalization.

Same corpus, two roles

Both methods see HowTo100M. Using it as a pseudo-label source for supervised fine-tuning (SFT-HT100M) degrades the base checkpoint at 3B and 7B, for example from 31.4 to 12.1 in-domain at 3B. Using the same corpus as a verifier for RL recovers and exceeds the base by 7.8 points in-domain and 16.3 zero-shot at 3B. At 3B and 7B both RECIPE variants (RL only and SFT→RL) outperform both SFT baselines, and RL only is the default scheme.

Robustness to weak supervision

We vary the fraction of weakly supervised training examples, where both history and continuation come from a VLM rather than from human annotations, from 0% to 100%. RECIPE-RL is flat across the supervision mix (36–40 in-domain, 35–39 zero-shot), while SFT peaks at intermediate mixes and collapses at the extremes, falling below the no-fine-tuning baseline zero-shot when annotations are scarce.

Fully unsupervised training

We remove curated procedural datasets from training entirely: histories are sampled as prefixes of HowToCaption narration sequences and the reward is computed against the same corpus, so all seven benchmarks are unseen. RECIPE-RL improves over Base at every scale (+21.5, +8.2, and +8.7 points), while SFT on the same data improves only the 0.5B model and degrades 3B and 7B.

Transfer to the Video configuration

With Qwen2.5-VL-3B reading video tokens directly, trained on CrossTask and evaluated zero-shot on NIV, RECIPE-RL improves over the base under both annotated and weak supervision, while SFT degrades the zero-shot benchmark by 7.9 points, mirroring the SFT collapse of the Socratic configuration. This is a single-backbone, targeted test rather than a full comparison.

More diverse and more accurate

A reward that accommodates many valid orderings should leave the policy free to express that variety. On 100 prompts from the COIN test split, with 10 generations per prompt, SFT collapses on both axes (lowest diversity and lowest rubric score), while RECIPE-RL is the only configuration in the upper-right region: it matches the base checkpoint's diversity (0.373 vs. 0.348) while adding nearly 10 rubric-score points.
